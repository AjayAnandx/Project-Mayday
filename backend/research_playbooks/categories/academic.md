## Question angles
- What the strongest studies actually measured and found, with effect sizes and their uncertainty.
- How the evidence was produced: designs, data, samples, benchmarks, and how far results generalise.
- Where studies disagree, and plausible reasons (method, population, data, era).
- What remains untested or contested, and what would settle it.

## What counts as strong evidence
- Peer-reviewed meta-analyses and systematic reviews; then well-designed experiments (randomised, quasi-experimental, controlled benchmarks); then observational studies.
- Results replicated by independent groups, on different data.
- Preprints are useful for recent work but are labelled as not yet peer reviewed.
- Retracted papers are never evidence.

## Pitfalls
- Citing a paper for a claim it only mentions in its introduction or related-work section.
- Reporting a headline number without its comparison, metric, dataset or sample.
- Treating benchmark gains as general capability, or correlation as causation.
- Mixing results measured with different metrics or definitions.
